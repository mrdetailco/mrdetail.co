Detailing 101

What Detailing Actually Does

A car wash makes a car look clean for a day. Detailing makes it clean — and protects it. Here's what each step does, why it matters, and how to know which one your car needs.

The Process

Every detail follows the same order for a reason: lift the loose dirt first, decontaminate what's bonded on, then protect the clean surface. Skip a step and you either scratch the paint or the protection won't hold.

Exterior

Foam pre-wash & rinse

A thick layer of foam and a pressure rinse lift loose grit and road film before anything touches the paint.

Most of the fine swirl marks you see under the sun come from rubbing dry dirt across the clear coat. Floating that grit off first means the wash isn't acting like sandpaper.

Exterior

Two-bucket contact wash

A wash mitt plus a separate rinse bucket, so grit comes off the mitt instead of going back onto the panel.

A drive-through brush or a single bucket drags the same dirt across your paint over and over — that dull haze on an older car is years of accumulated wash marring. Doing it by hand, the right way, is how you avoid adding to it.

Exterior

Wheels, wells & tires

Dedicated cleaner and brushes for the dirtiest, most-skipped part of the car.

Brake dust is iron, and it's mildly corrosive. Left sitting, it pits and stains the wheel finish over time — so wheels get their own attention, not a quick spray.

Decontamination

Iron decontamination

A chemical that dissolves the tiny metal specks — brake dust and rail fallout — bonded into the paint that washing can't lift.

Those specks rust from the inside out and leave the surface rough. Pulling them keeps the finish smooth and stops staining before it starts. Included on the Premium detail.

Decontamination

Clay bar

Clay glides over a lubricated surface and physically pulls out what's bonded on top of the clear coat — overspray, tar flecks, sap residue.

Run your hand over "clean" paint and you'll often feel grit. That's contamination sitting on the surface. Claying takes it to glass-smooth — which is what makes paint actually feel clean, and it's the step that lets the next layer of protection bond properly.

Exterior

Drying

Soft, high-GSM towels and air — drying without dragging anything across the surface.

Letting a car air-dry etches mineral water spots into the finish, and a rough towel can put in more marring than the wash ever did. The drying matters as much as the washing.

Protection

Sealant & protection

A hydrophobic layer goes down over the clean surface — a spray sealant on a Full Detail, or an upgrade to a longer-lasting ceramic sealant.

This is the part a cheap wash skips entirely. Protection shields against UV fade, water spots and bird droppings, makes the next wash easier, and is the reason water beads up and rolls off. A spray sealant lasts weeks; a ceramic sealant holds for months. A multi-year ceramic coating is a separate service, by quote.

Interior

Vacuum, extract, steam & dress

Full vacuum, hot-water extraction on carpets and cloth, steam and all-purpose cleaner on hard surfaces, glass done last.

A wipe-down just moves dust around. Extraction and steam pull out what's ground into the fibers — the source of most stains, smells and the grime packed into vents and seams. Glass goes last so nothing resettles on it.

Which Detail Do I Need?

Maintenance

Already in good shape

A reset for a car that's kept up — wash, vacuum and wipe-down to bring it back to clean. It's upkeep, not a rescue for built-up grime.

Every 2–4 weeks to stay ahead of it.

Full Detail

The popular one

The complete clean, inside and out — with decontamination and a fresh coat of protection. The right call if it's been a while, or the first time bringing a car back.

Every 3–4 months, or each season.

Premium

The deep one

Everything in Full, plus heavy-soil washing, iron decontamination included, and deep stain and spill work inside. For neglected cars, heavy interiors, or prepping to sell or show.

Once or twice a year, or a one-time reset.

How often should I detail?

A garage-kept weekend car needs far less than a daily driver that lives outside under trees and sun. The simple rhythm: maintain regularly, full detail a few times a year, and reach for a premium when it's gotten away from you.

Keeping up beats one big rescue — when protection stays fresh, dirt sits on the sealant instead of the paint and comes off easier every single time. That's cheaper for you and better for the car.
